The first article entails a re-visitation of the tenets of the CAP Theorem, and how new methods have manifested over the years to meet increased networking demands that have resulted in unconventional methodologies and utilities, at least in the context of the original theory. The initial formulation of the theorem presented a case where only two out of three conditions (consistency, availability, partition tolerance) could be adequately satisfied in distributed networks, however the article indicates that the statement may be an anachronistic oversimplification. Acknowledged distributed systems in the modern context, it is evident that fully optimized consistency and availability are not feasible in the context of distributed systems that exist in reality; instead, prioritization and management of system features must be managed accordingly to satisfy the requirements and functional expectations of the system.
