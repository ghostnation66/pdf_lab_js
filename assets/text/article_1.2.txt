The utilities of a scheme involving eventual consistency must be accompanied with a supporting functionality that involves either resilience to partition disparities during recovery, or compensation in circumstances where externalized mistakes are generated and affects the front-end user experience. The consequences of violating invariants may be determined by the cost of compensation or effect on the service's reliability, in some cases, a restoration strategy may simply involve merging of partitions and evaluation of any consideration that must be addressed post-recovery. Some services may benefit from a bounded-risk protocol in which incurrences of violations are permitted within certain limitations. In other cases, externalization of errors is best addressed by notifying the user and providing compensatory remuneration in the event of an inconsistency that retrospectively modifies the service's user commitment.
