Fundamentals and Neural Network Architecture
In order to implement optimizers within neural network training schemes, we must first define the effect of such techniques relative to unoptimized frameworks. Neural networks that do not optimize and use Vanilla Gradient Descent (VGD) have a stationary, predetermined learning rate (\(\alpha\)) that influences the degree to which a loss gradient contribute to the weight update step in backpropagation. Optimized learning methods implement a protocol by which the learning rate is modified based on the dynamics of the loss gradient; these methods typically utilize a momentum-based concept to influence the learning rate, in which the network is provided with introspection of the cumulative change of previous gradients (analogous to the "integral" term in PID control), and utilizes the information to accelerate or decelerate learning at specific regions of the loss landscape. In general, when large gradients are encountered, the learning rate will be downscaled to allow for more intensive training in a particular region exhibiting minimizing potential, so as to not overstep a potential minimum; the opposite is true for small gradients, where the learning rate is augmented to increase the traversal through the loss landscape where the loss is less dynamic, and can be "skipped" via large learning steps in order to more quickly encounter larger gradients. The implementation of this "self-optimizing" learning rate was initially applied to SGD networks (the stochasticity alludes to the batching of input data to compute incremental gradients) by Polyak in 1964; a velocity term is applied to a weight update. The velocity term itself is influenced by the gradient term, and characterizes the "resistance" of the weight change to an experience of the gradient. The association is provided below:
1
SGD with momentum
$$ \mathbf{w}_{t+1} = \mathbf{w_t} + \mathbf{v}_{t+1} $$
$$ \mathbf{v}_{t+1} = \rho \mathbf{v}_t - \mu \nabla \mathbf{w}_t $$
This "classical" momentum (which defines the weights as "particles in space") was improved by Nesterov via the implementation of "look-ahead" velocity, which evaluates \(\mathbf{v}_{t+1} = \rho \mathbf{v}_t - \mu \nabla (\mathbf{w}_t + \mathbf{v}_t ) \); With this parametrization, the weights are adjusted based upon a successor velocity state, allowing for more predictive adjustment of weight updates. Adaptive gradient methods (AdaGrad) utilize an expression that defines an operation for modulating the learning rate with respect to the gradient, permitting larger step sizes when the gradient is small, and forcing a reduction in the learning rate when the gradient grows, preventing an overshoot of potential minima. The equations in Box 2 depcit the evaluation of succesor weight during each iteration of training in an AdaGrad training scheme, the first showing no veolcity implementation, the second showing a velocity implementation
2
Adaptive Gradient (AdaGrad) without \(v\) $$ \omega_{i, t+1} = \omega_{i, t} - \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon + \sum_{j=1}^{t} g(\omega_{i, j})^2}} g(\omega_{i, t}) $$ Adaptive Gradient (AdaGrad) with \(v\) $$ \mathbf{v}_{t+1} = \mathbf{v}_{t} + g(\mathbf{w}_{t})^2 $$ $$ \mathbf{w}_{t+1} = \mathbf{w}_{t} - \frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon + \mathbf{v}_{t+1}}} g(\mathbf{w}_{t}) $$
AdaGrad suffers from an egregious impairment that involves an asymptotically decaying learning rate due to the summatrion of cumulative gradients. In order to allow a network to have temporarily persistent memeory, RMSProp was developed to minimize the magnitude of the "integrator" term, along the training schedule, reforumlating the velocity update term with a discount factor \(\eta\), as shown in Box 3. The coefficient on \(v\) is not merely discounting, but obtianing a weighted average of the gradient and velocity term: if \(\beta\) is large, then the system will be "heavy", resistant to changes in the gradient, and vice-versa for the opposite case.
3
RMSProp Discounted Velocity $$ \mathbf{v}_{t+1} = \beta \mathbf{v}_{t} + (1 - \beta) g(\mathbf{w}_{t})^2 $$
The Adam optimizer was developed to address the shortcomings of RMSProp, and applies the classical momentum reformulation to the gradient term. Aptly named, the Adam optimizer provides adaptable reconfiguration of the moments of the gradient, as opposed to gradients themselves, resulting in smoothing of potentially unproductive oscialltory motion along eigenvectors of the weight matrix that do not move aggressively toward a minimum.
